# REFERENCES.md — Phase 0b Reference Study

Fetched 2026-08-03 (WebFetch, text/markup layer; visual details cross-checked against the
brief's per-site guidance §5). Absorb technique and quality bar; copy nothing literally.
Note: several sites are JS-heavy — fetched content captured copy/structure reliably,
visual/motion detail partially; where the text layer was thin, bullets lean on the
brief's stated guidance and are marked accordingly.

---

## 1. LuxAlgo (luxalgo.com) — the competitor

- **Eyebrow-label grammar, confirmed live:** `Quant`, `Backtest · The evidence`,
  `Alerts · The autopilot`, `The Library · The foundation`, `pinets — quick start`.
  Exactly the pattern our copy already uses ("The Lab · What it does"). Absorb: mono
  small-caps eyebrow + short declarative heading ("Built to Understand Markets.") on
  every section — one shell, site-wide.
- **A live product mock in EVERY feature section:** hero = live TSLA chart in a framed
  "My Workspace" surface; Backtest section = a real results card (equity curve, net
  P&L bars, All/Long/Short split: "+$64,733", "61.4%", "1.64", "−6.8%"); Alerts
  section = a timestamped notification log. Nothing is illustrated abstractly — every
  section proves itself with product. This is their strongest pattern; ours in our skin.
- **Numbers as hero content:** their backtest card leads with big stats and lets labels
  whisper — validates our evidence-language direction (brief §3.1), except theirs are
  cherry-picked; ours must be real and include losers.
- **CTA verb discipline:** one product verb ("Launch Quant") repeated; secondary CTAs
  route to /pricing. Absorb the repetition of a single primary action, not the wording.
- **Terse 3-word closer section:** "Build. Backtest. Execute." mirrors our "Build it.
  Test it. Prove it in public." — confirms a short triad closer section works.
- **Do NOT take:** the identity — full-rainbow ring material, mystique/"edge/autopilot"
  vocabulary, prediction overlays ("Exp. Upper (+1 SD)"), hero win-rate stats. Their
  brand is dream-selling; ours is receipts.

## 2. Linear (linear.app) — restraint gold standard

- **~90% monochrome, one accent:** the whole page reads in neutrals with a single link/
  CTA accent. Premium = fewer colors used consistently, not more. Our budget: canvas +
  3 text tones + 1 accent + semantics.
- **Numbered section labels (1.0 Intake … 5.0 Monitor):** a pedagogical ordering device
  that makes a complex product feel like a guided sequence — a cousin of our eyebrow
  shell; consider `01 · The Lab` numbering inside the eyebrow.
- **Copy density ~30% of typical SaaS:** headings 3–5 words, body 1–2 sentences,
  whitespace does the persuading. We keep our copy verbatim but SET it this way: short
  display line, one-sentence subcopy, air around everything.
- **Screenshots sit inside narrative sections,** not in gallery blocks — contextual,
  cropped, doing one job each. Our chart demos should be framed the same way.
- **Weight discipline:** hierarchy from size/spacing, nothing shouts bold. Medium
  (500–600) display weights only.
- **Do NOT take:** the light-first theme and the purple-adjacent Linear-clone look the
  brief explicitly bans; also their information density is for a PM tool audience —
  don't ape the numbered-workflow structure literally.

## 3. Mercury (mercury.com) — trustworthy with money

- **Compliance woven in, benefits first:** numbered inline footnotes ("up to 3.83%
  yield"¹ style), full legal in footer, qualifiers immediately adjacent to claims.
  Model for our disclaimers: always present, typographically quiet, never hidden —
  matches our honesty-is-brand stance.
- **Weight variation carries hierarchy** (light body vs medium display, per fetch:
  "light body copy contrasts with bold section headers") — the "weight-480-class"
  premium feel the brief cites. No color needed to create importance.
- **ONE accent reserved for the primary action** (brief guidance; fetch shows a
  restrained blue/green accent world). Everything else is graphite and text tones.
- **Flat, borderless-or-hairline cards** (brief §2.1 cites Mercury's `#171721`-class
  near-black): depth from subtle elevation steps, never drop shadows. This is our
  surface recipe.
- **Testimonial/feature imagery is calm and editorial,** alternating text/image
  sections with generous whitespace — the rhythm to copy for our feature sections.
- **Do NOT take:** the current homepage's light-mode illustration style (abstract
  shapes, flowing lines) — we are dark-first and product-real, not illustrated.

## 4. Vercel (vercel.com) — ambient engineering

- **Ambient glow as a background LAYER:** asset names literally ship as
  "fallback-dark-glow-desktop" — a soft radial glow behind hero content with a static
  image fallback. Exactly the hero treatment for us: accent radial glow + faint grid,
  with a no-JS/reduced-motion static fallback.
- **Theme-paired product screenshots** (dark/light × mobile/desktop variants) — for us,
  dark-only simplifies this: one crisp dark screenshot set, mobile variant reserved.
- **Grid as texture:** multi-column grids and hairline rules give an instrumented,
  engineered feel without decoration — supports our faint-grid/scanline ambient
  texture (§3.1), barely perceptible.
- **"New" badges as the only accent pops** in dense nav/link lists — accent used as
  metadata, not paint. Same discipline for our "verified/frozen" badges.
- **Case-study-driven sections** (Notion/Zapier/Mintlify each get one section, one
  claim, one visual) — one proof object per section, never two.
- **Do NOT take:** pure-black + monochrome austerity; we need slightly warmer
  near-black and one real accent, or we read as a Vercel clone.

## 5. Raycast (raycast.com) — vibrant dark, tasteful

- **Glow halos around interactive elements only** — luminance signals "you can touch
  this," not decoration. Maps directly to our gradient ring rules (§3.2): rings on
  hover/focus, one idle hero CTA max.
- **Glass-morphism used sparingly on feature panels** ("Snippets blue glass") — subtle
  backdrop-blur on floating surfaces (our sticky nav, chat dock), never on everything.
- **Modular visual moments instead of one monolithic demo:** keyboard visual anchors
  four one-word claims ("Fast, Ergonomic, Native, Reliable"). Pattern for our metric
  tiles: one visual, few whispered labels.
- **Emotional reframe line** ("It's not about saving time…") set as a standalone
  full-width statement between feature sections — our "Every opinion this AI has is a
  backtest" deserves this exact treatment.
- **Category tabs to gate volume** (Productivity/Engineering/Design…) — progressive
  disclosure for big libraries; pattern for /library filter chips.
- **Do NOT take:** the playful multi-color gradient personality and 12-card feature
  carousels — too many simultaneous vibrant moments for a trust-first trading product.

## 6. Reflect (reflect.app) — the glow ceiling

- **One luminous focal point per viewport** (hero orb/video is THE light source; the
  rest of the page stays dark) — the discipline that keeps HD-glow from becoming noise.
  Our rule: never more than one idle-animating/glowing element in view (§3.2, §7).
- **Reveal-on-interaction moments** ("Click to see magic") — motion as reward for
  intent, not autoplay everywhere. Matches our hover-triggered ring rotation.
- **Dark canvas as atmosphere, content as light:** bright timestamped UI mockups
  (calendar rows, meeting notes "5:00am / 7:00am") pop against near-black — exactly
  how our ledger rows and ticket-stub timestamps should read.
- **Premium restraint in copy scale:** short declaratives ("Think better with
  Reflect") under orchestrated visuals — the visual is the argument.
- **Do NOT take:** the full orchestrated-orb cinematic — it's a notes app selling
  wonder; we're a research instrument selling proof. Borrow atmosphere at ~30%
  intensity, never at the cost of legibility of numbers.

## 7. Resend (resend.com) — how simple polished can be

- **One-sentence self-definition as the hero:** "Resend is the email API for
  developers." No slogan gymnastics. Confirms our hero can be headline + one line +
  proof object + CTA, done.
- **Documentation-grade sparseness:** heading weight and spacing alone differentiate
  sections; near-zero decoration. The floor to check against: if a section still works
  Resend-bare, added decoration must justify itself (Chanel rule, §0.5).
- **Zero motion is still credible** — the reduced-motion version of our site should
  feel like this: complete, calm, intentional. Design the static state first.
- **Developer-trust through structure** (specs, changelogs, plain URLs) — parallels
  our receipts identity: expose the spec hash, the timestamp, the raw record.
- **Do NOT take:** this level of minimalism for marketing pages — our landing must
  persuade skeptical traders with proof objects, not read like docs. (Fetch also hit a
  text-index variant of the page; visual bullets here are direction, not observation.)

## 8. Composer (composer.trade) — closest product category

- **Conversation → strategy → results as ONE visual narrative:** their AI demo shows
  the user's plain-English request, the generated strategy visualization, then a
  benchmark-compared performance table + cumulative-return line. This exact
  three-beat (ask → spec → evidence) is our playground story; frame our live demo
  the same way.
- **Benchmark comparison everywhere:** every backtest visual pairs strategy vs SPY.
  Ours already does this — keep it prominent; it IS the honesty language.
- **Progressive disclosure sequencing:** outcome-first hero ("No coding skills
  required") → pre-built strategies BEFORE editor internals → technical customization
  last. Homepage order lesson: curated templates before any spec/JSON depth (kills the
  14-card wall rationale: they curate categories, never dump the library).
- **Contextual compliance:** "With any investment, your capital is at risk…" inline
  near claims, asterisked fee footnotes, disclosure links at point of relevance — a
  live example of compliance-forward fintech marketing that still converts.
- **Editor shown as isolated component cards** (weighting, conditionals, filters as
  individual mock cards) rather than full-app screenshots — how to preview the Lab
  without overwhelming.
- **Do NOT take:** their light, friendly-retail visual identity and stat-flexing
  ("$37B+ volume") social proof — we don't have those numbers and our tone is
  evidence-quiet, not scale-loud.

## 9. Stripe (stripe.com) — gradient and transition craft

- **Signature gradient with a static fallback** ("wave-fallback-desktop.png"): the
  animated hero material degrades to an image. Our conic-ring + hero glow must ship
  the same way (reduced-motion → static gradient, §3.2).
- **Gradient as ONE hero material, not a site-wide wash:** the wave lives in the hero;
  sections below return to calm neutrals. Our accent-spectrum ring is likewise a
  special material, rationed (§2.1).
- **Predictable section rhythm:** hero → logo proof → capability grid → stats →
  audience-split paths → developer section → freshness → footer, separated by
  whitespace and heading scale, not dramatic background flips. Identical vertical
  rhythm token across all sections (§2.3).
- **Stats section as credibility anchor** ("135+ currencies… 99.999% uptime") — big
  tabular numbers, tiny labels: our evidence-tile pattern at marketing scale.
- **Bento-grid product imagery** with background-image screenshots — a way to show
  several product moments in one composed, calm block.
- **Do NOT take:** the light theme and the multi-hue gradient itself — steal the
  craft (fallbacks, rationing, transition smoothness), never the palette.

## 10. Supabase (supabase.com) — one accent = brand

- **A single unexpected accent OWNS the brand** in a dark developer category — green
  on near-black is instantly "Supabase" from a thumbnail. Proof that our one-accent
  bet (electric cyan / cold amber / ice blue candidate set, §2.1 — green is banned
  for us) can carry the entire identity if we never dilute it.
- **Accent as semantic marker, not paint:** CTAs, active states, terminal prompts —
  large surfaces stay graphite. Where the accent does NOT appear is what makes it work.
- **Monospace as brand voice:** `Postgres`, `pgvector`, `Row Level Security` set in
  mono inline — technical precision AS typography. Ours: every number, hash, and
  timestamp in mono/tabular (§2.2, §3.1).
- **Uniform feature-card grid:** six capabilities, identical card anatomy (bold title +
  1–2 sentence description) — the consistency-over-cleverness template for our
  template/strategy cards.
- **Precision numbers as trust** ("40+ preinstalled extensions", "16+ global
  regions") — exact counts beat adjectives. Our equivalents: "N strategies", "N days
  live", real run counts.
- **Do NOT take:** green (reserved for P&L semantics in our product) and the
  docs-adjacent density of their feature lists on marketing surfaces.

## Synthesis — techniques this redesign adopts

1. **Universal section shell with mono eyebrow labels** (LuxAlgo's `X · Y` grammar +
   Linear's terse headings + Stripe's fixed vertical rhythm) → brief §3.3, §2.3. One
   wrapper — eyebrow, heading, subcopy, content slot, single CTA — on every marketing
   section, identical spacing tokens site-wide.
2. **One-accent discipline on near-black graphite** (Supabase's brand-through-accent +
   Mercury's one-action reservation + Linear's ~90% monochrome budget) → brief §2.1.
   Accent appears ONLY on primary CTAs, active states, and the signature ring; flat
   hairline cards, no drop shadows.
3. **Live product proof object in every feature section** (LuxAlgo's strongest pattern,
   via Linear's contextual-crop framing and Composer's ask→spec→evidence narrative) →
   brief §4-homepage. Real chart, real ledger rows, real stats — never illustration.
4. **Evidence typography: mono/tabular numbers as heroes, labels whisper** (Supabase
   mono-as-voice + Stripe stat anchors + Reflect's bright-data-on-dark ledger look) →
   brief §2.2, §3.1. Extends to timestamps, truncated hashes, verified badges — all
   encoding true data.
5. **Rationed signature glow/ring: one luminous element per viewport** (Reflect's
   single focal light + Raycast's interactive-only halos + LuxAlgo's ring energy, in
   our 2–3-stop accent spectrum) → brief §3.2. Idle animation on the hero CTA only;
   everything else hover/focus; reduced-motion → static.
6. **Static-first fallbacks for every effect** (Stripe's wave-fallback + Vercel's
   glow-fallback assets + Resend's zero-motion credibility) → brief §6. The
   no-animation site must look finished; effects are progressive enhancement.
7. **Progressive disclosure of the library** (Composer's curated-before-technical
   ordering + Raycast's category tabs) → brief §4 template section: 6 curated cards +
   "Explore all N →", filter chips on /library, spec depth only on detail views.
8. **Ambient instrumentation texture: faint grid + hero radial glow with dark product
   screenshots** (Vercel's grid/glow layers) → brief §3.1, §4-hero. Barely
   perceptible, one hero glow, never competing with data.
